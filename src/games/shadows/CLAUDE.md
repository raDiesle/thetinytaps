# Erkennen (Schatten finden + Wer schaut da raus?)

Route `/schatten/` (kept, so no address changes). Since November 2026 (Z-14, user decision) the game is called „Erkennen“ (`names.ts`, German only for now) and has two kinds of rounds taking turns: a picture looks for its shadow, then an animal peeks out of the bush or pond („Wer schaut da raus?“, moved here from „Was gehört zum Tier?“ with all its scenes), then a picture again … (`advance` in `ShadowsPlay.tsx`). Each kind climbs its own steps (`SHADOW_LEVELS`, `PEEK_LEVELS`) by `nextLevel` on its own rounds. Only with the picture kinds mixed or Tiere do animals peek out; with Fahrzeuge, Essen or Dinge only shadows come.

Its card plays at once (`startMode`): there is nothing to choose before playing, and ✕ goes back to the table of contents, never to a start screen with only „Los geht’s!“. A picture looks for its shadow among 2–6 black ones (the same emoji with `filter: brightness(0)`); tap the right shadow or drag the picture onto it, or choose with arrow keys + Enter / 1–6. A wrong shadow shows its colours for a moment (spoken: "Das ist die Giraffe."). Pictures and texts in `content.ts`, by theme (Tiere, Fahrzeuge, Essen, Dinge).

**Difficulty** (`SHADOW_LEVELS`, `nextLevel`, `dealShadows`, `poseShadows` in `levels.ts`, like Socken-Paare): it begins with 2 shadows of different kinds (an animal among a car and a banana), then 3, then 3 of the same kind (`alike`), up to 6. Then come new ways to look (user feedback, November 2026: harder, and the game decides by itself when): the shadows lie **turned** (`turn`: each by half to all of its degrees, to either side), then some **mirrored** too (`mirror`, at least one), and on the last steps they lie **under paper flaps** like memory cards (`covered`): a tap lifts one flap, hinged at its left like a page, and shows its shadow (the one lifted before goes down again), a tap on a lifted shadow picks it, a drag onto a covered one lifts it; a still hand points at the first flap until one was lifted that visit, and a covered shadow's name waits under its flap. Each new way starts again with fewer shadows, so it is something new to learn, not a wall (`shadows.test.ts`). The picture landing on a turned or mirrored shadow turns with it to fit. After each picture, from the wrong shadows tapped in the pictures on the current step: on the first steps (`QUICK_STEPS`) one picture without one → one up, after that two in a row; two in one picture, or one in each of two pictures in a row → one down. Time plays no part (a slow but sure child climbs too). Every visit begins on the first step; `?level=N` starts on step N while developing.

**Kind of pictures** („Was drankommt“): mixed at the start of every visit (not saved). It is chosen right in the top right corner while playing, a small paper note with the choices in words – Gemischt, Tiere, Fahrzeuge, Essen, Dinge – the chosen one marked by the highlighter's stroke (`CornerChoices`, the same note as Was gehört zum Tier?'s; user request, November 2026, replacing an emoji button in the top left that opened a row of emoji). It stays there in every round, also while an animal peeks out. Picking one brings a new picture of it at once, on the same step. With one kind only, `alike` makes no difference.

„Wer schaut da raus?“ (idea X-04, `PeekPlay.tsx`, steps `PEEK_LEVELS` and `dealPeek` in `levels.ts`): the second kind of round, sharing the stage, pictures and glyphs with the shadows: only a part of an animal peeks out of a bush (top, left or right) or the pond (`hideouts.tsx`) – nothing stands beside it (a fox or bear family pointing at the bush was decoration and was taken out, user feedback, November 2026) –, or on the hardest steps shows only through gaps in the bush's leaves (`pk-hole`); the child taps the whole animal among 2–5 below, and it comes out; tapping the bush rustles it and the animal makes its noise. The emoji is drawn on a canvas and cut to its outline (`glyph.ts`, which also measures where it is drawn for the gaps), so the part that shows is the same on every system. The rules below are for the shadows only.

## Rules

- Nothing to read for the child while playing: no question, no numbers, nothing on a wrong shadow. Only for grown-ups,
  each shadow (and each whole animal in „Wer schaut da raus?“) has its name small and quiet below it (`.sh-name`, the
  picture's name from `texts.name`, in the app language), one line, centred (user feedback, November 2026).
- The shadows are as big as the meadow below the picture allows – 2 to 6 in a row (2 or 3 to a row on a phone held
  upright, `choiceRows` in `levels.ts`), clear of the page's paper margin, with room kept below for their names
  (`--name-room`); sizes come from the meadow as a size container (`--size` in `shadows.css`), never from fixed `vh`
  caps (user feedback, November 2026: the buttons were small with room to spare).
- No options: no start screen before playing and no settings. Difficulty comes from `nextLevel`, the kind of pictures from the note in the top right corner while playing – the user asked for both. Words only there, no emoji, and no ⚙️ or panel.
- A wrong shadow never shows or sounds as wrong; it only feeds `nextLevel`.
- Harder means more shadows, closer outlines and new ways to look (turned, mirrored, under flaps to remember), never quicker: no clock, and time doesn't count for the difficulty. A new way to look goes in as steps of `SHADOW_LEVELS`, after the ones it builds on, starting with fewer shadows; lifting a flap is never a wrong pick.
- Exactly one shadow fits (X-14): pictures whose black shadows can't be told apart on Apple's or Google's emojis
  (pig, sheep and turtle on Android, cat and dog on an iPhone, apple and strawberry, mushroom and tree) are listed in
  `SHADOW_LOOKALIKES` and never dealt together; `shadows.test.ts` checks every step. Closer outlines make a step harder
  only while each can still be told apart. A new picture is looked at in black next to the pictures closest to it, in
  both fonts (Noto Color Emoji from Google Fonts in the preview), and joins the list where it is one of them; the same
  goes for „Wer schaut da raus?“ (`PEEK_LOOKALIKES`: what peeks out, in colour, at the hardest share and through the
  gaps – the cat's and the dog's tail, grey elephant, cow and sheep, grey-white rabbit and mouse). Plain fur of one
  colour is what gives animals away least through the gaps: the brown and tan ones (monkey, deer, kangaroo, camel, goat,
  horse), the grey ones and the green ones are paired there. A new animal goes into exactly one `PEEK_LOOKS` group and,
  when it lives in water, into `IN_THE_POND`; only whole bodies, never a face or a round blob (🐤 and 🐸 are faces). Of two animals that are the same shape (tiger and leopard, camel and dromedary, dolphin and shark) take one.
- Shapes that fail most often in black (measured November 2026 on about 190 emojis, `research.ts`), so look at a new
  picture against these first: Apple's vehicles seen at an angle from the front are all one box (bus, lorry, fire
  engine, tram, police car – an ambulance would be a sixth, so it was left out); food in a box or a cup is a box
  (chips, popcorn, cup, juice); a round head on a short stem is one shape (fried egg, lollipop, balloon, light bulb,
  tree, peacock); shoes and socks are one leg with a foot; most fruit, vegetables, bread and sweets are round blobs and
  stay out. An emoji drawn half see-through (🪼 on Apple) gives a grey shadow, not a black one: leave it out. Of two
  alike, keep the one a small child knows better (the gondola went, the cable car stayed). Only things a small child
  knows from its own day, never signs, letters, numbers or faces.
- A German name in the plural (die Trauben, die Kirschen, die Spaghetti, die Handschuhe) is wrapped in `many(…)` in
  `content.ts`, so its sentences say „suchen“, „haben“, „sind“ (`shadows.test.ts` checks the sentences).

## Languages

Both games speak the app language (German, English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic or Russian, `useAppLanguage()`): start screen texts inline with `t({ de, en, zh, hi, es, ar, ru })`, game texts through `pick(SHADOW_TEXTS, language.id)` (`ShadowTexts` in `content.ts`). Every sentence is said from its ElevenLabs recording (`voice.ts`, files in `stimme/`), recorded in every app language
(November 2026); a German fallback sentence (a new one not translated yet) is silent in another language
(`recordedVoice` never plays the German one for another language). A new or changed sentence is listed by `src/content/recordedLines.ts` (`schatten`) and recorded with
`bun run voice make --only schatten`, only when the user asks. „Wer schaut da raus?“ speaks with Was gehört zum Tier?'s recordings (`animalsVoice`, files in the animals' `stimme/`). With ≈540 lines per language the game never loads all of them (`loadAll`): a round shows only once the line it starts with is loaded (`useVoiceReady` in `ShadowsPlay`; the paw track shows meanwhile, picture and question come together), then the rest of its lines load, and the next picture and the next animal are drawn from their decks a turn early (`Ahead`, same order as before) so their lines load while this round is played; their step and the others to choose from are dealt only when they come. A new line a round says goes into those lists. Each other language is a `content.<lang>.ts` (en, zh, hi, es, ar, ru), listed in `src/content/texts/texts-<lang>.ts` (which fills `SHADOW_TRANSLATIONS`): the pictures' names by id in the shape its grammar needs – English and Spanish `many` for plural words („scissors are“, „las tijeras buscan“), Spanish the article („del elefante“); Hindi the oblique form before की (कछुए की) and sentences without gendered verbs („… की परछाईं कहाँ है?“, „यह … है।“); Arabic `female` for the verb and pronoun (يبحث عن ظله / تبحث عن ظلها) and `the` where ال can't simply go in front; Russian the genitive for „Вот тень слона!“ and `many` („ножницы ищут“).
- Translations come only when the user asks. A new or changed German line comes German-only; add the other languages only on request. Gaps are marked in `src/content/ideas/languageStatus.ts`.
- A new picture comes German only: its id goes on `GERMAN_ONLY` in `content.ts` (the list is empty now: the 114 pictures added in November 2026 – the animals of „Wer schaut da raus?“, more vehicles, food and things – were translated), `shadows.test.ts` checks that the translated languages name every other picture and none of these yet, and the Schatten finden row in `languageStatus.ts` says what is missing. Once the user asks for their translations, they come off the list (a Hindi name may carry `many` for a plural word like दस्ताने).
- When a German line changes its meaning, delete its translations in the same change (a stale translation is worse than German).
- A picture without its name in a language is said entirely in German as text (the language's texts function takes the whole German sentence; `say` finds no recording of it in another language and stays silent). `shadows.test.ts` checks that no id is stale, that the languages translated so far name every picture, that Russian, Chinese, Hindi and Arabic have no Latin letters, and that the fallback works.
- Sentences must not use the past tense or depend on the picture's gender where the language can avoid it (no „нашёл/нашла“, no „его/её“; Hindi without रहा/रही); use the stored forms.
- Never show `item.name` directly; it is German. Use `texts.name(item)`.
