# Trail adventures („Weg-Abenteuer“, `trail/`)

A long world seen from the side that scrolls along with the child (far hills slower, for depth). Something the child loves goes away at the start (the kite flies off), and the child follows it. The action button lights up when the child can walk on: its frame turns yellow, the button red, it breathes and sends out rings. A tap on it, anywhere, or any key walks to the next station (after a while it jumps and the footprints and the ghost come again – never the goal: the thought bubble shows the goal only when it goes away). The button lights up only where the child gets to do something there – take a thing, a `moment`, the stairs: to an obstacle or a fork the child walks on by itself, since a tap that only walks is no fun. A tap (or key) while something is still going on is kept (`queued`): the button stays lit, and at the next station the footprints and the ghost show for a moment (`QUEUED_MS`) and the child walks on by itself – a kept tap is dropped where the child has to pick a thing or a way. Before the tap the child can see what it will do: yellow footprints lead to the child's pale ghost, standing where the tap takes it and doing what it will do there (reaching for the thing, standing at the obstacle, on the top step), and the button shows the thing it gets there, or feet (walk to a moment), or stairs (a climb). A thing the tap picks up has a dashed yellow ring round it in the world, and waits pale in the backpack on the screen, ringed the same, over the place where it will go (`WantRing`). The child wears a red backpack (`Backpack` in `../kid.tsx`, the `backpack` prop of `Kid`), and the same backpack is drawn big and open on the screen – its red body is the frame, with the handle sticking out on top and the lid folded open: it has three pockets side by side, open at the top, each holding one thing (a long one like the plank slanted) behind a raised front like a button (with a buckle, or its small key cap on a computer); an empty pocket shows a dashed place. While the child picks, each pocket holding a thing has a breathing yellow frame like the waiting action button, and the pocket whose thing comes out is pressed in. A thing lying on the way: the child takes it (pose `take`), holds it up high (`lift`), the backpack's lid swings open and the thing goes over the shoulder into it while the other hand reaches back (`pack`), the backpack bounces, and on the screen the thing drops into the big backpack. At an obstacle a thought bubble says why a thing is needed (`why` of the station): a creature thinks of what it wants (the ducks of the bread, the bubble above its head, growing away from the child), or the child thinks of what would happen (`worries.tsx`: wet under the sprinkler, in the water at the stream or the hole in the bridge, in the dark, the ball dug out of the sand, bare feet on hot sand, stuck in a snow drift, slipping on ice) – a big frowning head, since the bubble is small. Then the things on the screen come up out of the backpack and the child taps the one that helps (or 1–3, or the creature's bubble, which shows the very thing – `tapBubble`): it rises out of the big backpack, the child takes it out of its own backpack, holds it up and throws it over, and it is used by itself. A wrong thing is tried: the obstacle wobbles and tosses its head three times with a funny sound, the thing comes back into the hand and the backpack, and the right one glows (it also glows after a while without a tap, and a light runs along the `why` bubble). Food is eaten where it can be seen: in the mouth (`tl-chew`), or at the beak, bite by bite (`tl-bites`) – the ducks peck the bread up on the path and then waddle into the pond. The carried thing moves frame by frame in world points (`moveCarry` in `TrailPlay.tsx`, its path from `carryAt` in `view.ts`), drawn in front of the child, or behind it while it sinks into the backpack or comes up out of it. Stairs take one tap, up all the steps (and down a slide, if there is one). A friend (G-10, `friends.tsx`): a creature the child has helped (`befriends` of an obstacle) hops and calls in its place, its picture – its head peeking out – drops into the backpack and takes a place there like a thing, and it runs round behind the child and follows it from then on along the way, round the turns too (it waits at the foot of stairs and runs along below a slide). At a later obstacle the child picks it like a thing – its picture in the backpack, 1–3, or the creature itself in the world: it barks, runs over and does the job itself (the dog digs), then comes back; a wrong try makes the obstacle shake its head and the friend comes back with its picture. Once it has helped its picture is gone from the backpack, but it stays with the child and jumps for joy at the end. `TrailPlay` draws the friend from the moment it joins, so the scenery leaves the creature out (`SceneryProps.friends`). At a fork (G-09) the way splits: two round picture signs on a signpost show the ways (stairs up, a tunnel), footprints lead to a ghost on each, and the child taps a sign or a ghost (or 1 / 2; any other tap or key makes the signs hop) – the action button stays dark, as there is nothing to walk on to yet. The picked way's first station starts right away, its own stations follow, and both ways meet again; next time the child can take the other one. At the end the goal is back in the hands, then „Nochmal“ and „Weiter“ with the level's trophy (nothing rises). Bag and button sit along the screen's bottom edge, under the child (`layoutHud` in `hud.ts`, drawn by `BagAndButton` in `layers.tsx`): the backpack at the screen's left edge (clear of a notch and a home bar), the button in its frame right next to it, where one thumb reaches both. The child's feet stand just above them (`Hud.feet`), so they never cover the child or the way, and the world moves up instead – the sky gets only what is left. A screen taller than the picture (a tablet) zooms the world in (`Hud.zoom`, at most `MAX_ZOOM`) rather than showing more sky, bag and button grow with it, and the child stands further left so `AHEAD` of the way stays in sight. The level drawings keep their 1600 × 900 picture with the feet at `KID_AT`; only the stage transform in `TrailPlay` moves and zooms it. `hud.test.ts` checks this for computer, tablet and phone. On a computer (not on touch-only devices) small key caps show the keys: a space bar under the action button and 1, 2, 3 on the backpack's front pockets, under the things; a cap turns yellow while its key does something and goes down while the key is held. Any key still walks on – the space bar is only the one shown.
- A world can go up instead of sideways (G-04, the stairwell): `turns` lists the points of the ground where the way turns round, and `placed`/`facing` in `ground.ts` draw it zigzagging up between x 0 and 1600 – each floor runs the other way, and the child faces the way it walks. Then the ground's x, `start`, the stations' `x` and the steps are the way walked, while every other point (`at`, `wants`, flights, the drawing) is where it is drawn; the camera keeps the whole width and follows only the height (the child's feet at 620 of 900, so there is room above). A goal with `turn` (the cat) faces the way it moves, and a `why` bubble grows away from the child, whichever way it faces.
- `types.ts` – the level format: `TrailLevel` with its ground line, stations (`pickup`, `obstacle`, `climb` with an optional `slide` down and a `sled` to ride it on, `moment` – something that happens by itself, and `fork` – two `ways`, each with its sign picture, its stations and optionally its own ground) and the goal's flights away and back (`away.thief` draws a creature carrying the goal off, like the hare with the carrot; `finale.into` makes the goal part of the world when it lands, like the nose on the snowman's face). On water a level has a `raft`: the child stands on it from the start and paddles (pose `paddle`, a paddle on the button and the ghost) up to `raft.until`, a climb where it gets off and the raft stays behind. An obstacle with `float` sends the child slowly down ground points by itself after it is solved, holding the thing up (the umbrella over the waterfall), then puts the thing down at `put` (the level draws it there from then on); `levels.test.ts` checks both lie on the ground. `ground.ts` walks the ground and lists the paths through a level (`pathsOf`).
- `levels.ts` – the levels' data (`TRAIL_LEVELS`); `levels.test.ts` checks it along every path through the level (bag never over three, every need is in the bag, stations left to right, steps on the ground, the last station frees the goal), and that a way's own ground matches the level's outside the way.
- `<level>.tsx` – one world drawing per level (`drachen.tsx`, `ball.tsx`): a `Scenery` that draws the stations from `done`/`here`/`nope` (and `party`, true once the goal is back – the sandcastle gets built; `friends`, the creatures walking along, which the scenery leaves out), a `Backdrop`, and optionally a `Front` drawn over the goal.
- What the child wears: `wear` of a level (bare feet at the beach) and of an obstacle that is solved by putting the thing on (the flip-flops on the hot sand) – the thing goes onto the child (`Outfit` in `../kid.tsx`), not over the obstacle.
- `items.tsx` – the bag's things and the goals, drawn standing on (0, 0); a goal says how it is carried (on a string above the child, or in the hands) and whether it rolls. A friend's picture is an `ItemId` too (`dog`), so it stands in the backpack like a thing.
- `friends.tsx` – the friends (`FRIENDS`): the creature in the world facing right, with its poses `sit`, `walk` (legs and tail move only while it runs) and `work` (its job), and its voice. `TrailPlay.tsx` – the script (it reads its refs, so it stays one component); `view.ts` – the state React draws (`View`, `startView`), what the next tap shows (`nextFrom`) and the paths goal and carried thing move along (`goalAt`, `carryAt`), no drawing, tested in `view.test.ts`; `hud.ts` – where bag, button and the child's feet go on the screen (`layoutHud`); `layers.tsx` – what is drawn besides the world: `Layer`, the backpack with the action button (`BagAndButton`), the fork signs (`ForkSigns`), rings and key caps; `trail.css` – classes `tl-` (`tl-ball-` for the ball level).
- The child (`Kid` from `../kid.tsx`), the thought bubble and the action button (`../house.tsx`) and their classes come from `../elevator.css`, which the game always loads.
- The picture is a stack of layers (`Layer` in `layers.tsx`), each its own svg in a `div.tl-layer` that CSS moves as a whole: the far hills, the world (scenery, goal, footprints, ghosts, signs, the thing behind the child), the friend, the child, what is in front of it (the carried thing, a creature's bubble), and bag and button on top. A walker (child, friend) sits in a second `div` inside the world's pan, drawn on (0, 0), so both moves run on the graphics card and stay in step. Where the child's feet stand on the screen and the zoom (`stage`, from `layoutHud`) are one fixed `<g>` inside each world layer's svg that changes only with the screen, so the pixels a layer moves by include the zoom. Never move the world, the camera or a walker as a group inside one svg: the browser then paints the whole picture again in every frame of a walk, which made walking stutter on phones and tablets. Something new goes into the layer where it belongs in the stacking; a layer takes taps only on what is drawn in it (`:where(.tl-layer svg > *)` in `trail.css`). The level's `Scenery`, `Front` and `Backdrop` are built again only when `done`, `here`, `nope`, `party` or the friend change (`useMemo`), never with each step, key or tap – keep what they get stable.

Levels so far:
- „Der Drachen“ (garden: ball, carrot, plank → dog wants the ball → lantern at the hut → horse wants the carrot → apple from the tree → plank over the stream → lantern in the dark tunnel → stairs up the hill → the squirrel trades the kite for the apple).
- „Der Ball rollt weg“ (G-02, the own street: the football bounces out of the hands and rolls away → watering can in the front garden → the child presses the button at the traffic light, it turns green, over the zebra crossing → a bone at the pet shop (paw on the sign, fish bowl and bird in the window) → the dog on the pavement wants the bone, eats it and comes along (G-10, its picture in the backpack) → in the park the sunflowers hang their heads over the path and want water (watering can or dog) → up the ladder and down the slide by itself, the dog runs below → the ball is half in the sand; the dog digs it out, the child throws it up and catches it). The shovel it once had was taken out: next to the dog it would have been a second right answer.
- „Der Luftballon“ (G-03, `ballon.tsx`, the town: bread from the bakery, fish from the market stall, sunflower from the flower stall → the ducks at the pond want the bread → umbrella on the park bench → the sprinkler sprays over the path, the umbrella goes over it → plank on the logs → the plank fills the hole in the hanging bridge → the cat wants the fish → long stairs up to the church → the dove on the tower trades the balloon for the sunflower).
- „Die kleine Lok“ (G-09, `lok.tsx`, the farm and the hill: the wooden toy train drives off by itself → carrot from the vegetable patch → corn cob at the corn field → the rabbit on the path wants the carrot → at the hill the way forks: **over** – up the stairs, apple from the tree on top, the pony wants the apple, down the path – or **through** – lantern from the post at the tunnel mouth, the dark tunnel wants the lantern → both meet at the gate of the hen yard, the child opens it → a hen sits on the train and hops down for the corn).
- „Das Floß“ (G-08, `floss.tsx`, the river: the toy sailboat drifts off → the child paddles after it on a raft (pose `paddle`) → saw at the woodpile → umbrella at the angler's spot → a branch hangs into the water, the saw cuts it off → stick under the willow → the mother duck looks for her ducklings, the reeds part and they come out (a `moment`) → at the waterfall the child opens the umbrella and floats slowly down (`float`), then sticks it into the raft as a sunshade → up the ladder onto the jetty, the raft stays behind → the beaver holds the boat and wants the stick for its lodge, gnaws it and lets go).
- „Die Sandburg“ (G-05, `sandburg.tsx`, the beach, the child barefoot: a wave takes bucket and spade along → shell in the sand → roll from the kiosk → the gull on the path wants the roll → flip-flops on the towel → air mattress at the beach hut → the sand is hot, the flip-flops go on the feet → steps up the rocks → the mattress bridges the water between the rocks → down the dune; a crab sits in the bucket and wants a house, it moves into the shell → everybody builds a sandcastle, the gull lands on it).
- „Picknick im Wald“ (G-07, `picknick.tsx`, the forest: the teddy runs off with the picnic basket → a hazelnut under the hazel bush → honey at the beehives → the squirrel wants the nut → blackberries on a twig → a hedgehog curls up while the child stands still (`moment`) → the robins want the berries and fly onto a branch → up the stones onto the log over the river → umbrella in the hut → the waterfall, the umbrella goes over it → the bear at the blanket holds the teddy and trades it for the honey; then the picnic comes out and the animals of the way come to eat along).
- „Wo ist die Katze?“ (G-04, `katze.tsx`, the stairwell going up, with `turns`: the cat jumps out of the arms and runs up the stairs → torch from the shelf in the hall → bone by the dog's bowl on the 1st floor → the dog sits on the stairs and wants the bone, trots down to its mat → the child rings at Oma's door on the 2nd floor, she waves and rolls out a ball of wool → up the ladder through the hatch → the dark attic wants the torch → the kittens in the basket want the wool and play; the cat comes back into the arms).
- „Der Schneemann“ (G-06, `schneemann.tsx`, snow: a hare takes the snowman's carrot nose and hops off → snow shovel at the house → seed sack at the shed → the shovel digs the path through the snow drift → sand bucket from the grit box → the birds want the seeds and fly up to the bird table to eat → cabbage from the vegetable bed → the sand is strewn on the frozen pond → up the snow steps, down the hill on the sled → the hare trades the carrot for the cabbage, it flies onto the snowman's face and he dances).
