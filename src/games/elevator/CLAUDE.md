# Kleine Abenteuer

Route `/kleine-abenteuer/` (the old `/aufzug/` redirects there) · ideas N-37 and area G („Kleine Abenteuer“), in „Lebendige Welten“. Was called „Spaß im Aufzug“; the folder, the code names (`Elevator…`) and the settings key `tinytaps.elevator.v2` keep the old name so saved settings and other branches still fit.

Picture adventures of a few minutes for 2–3-year-olds. Two kinds of levels, picked on the start screen (each card has a small tag for the parent, „🛗 Aufzug“, „🚶 Laufen“ or „🛶 Paddeln“, the `kind` of `GameMode`); at the end „Menü“ goes back to the start screen, „Nochmal“ plays the same one again and „Weiter“ (with the next one's picture, any key too) goes on to the next in `CARDS` order, after the last back to the first (`cards.tsx`, the shared `GameEnd`):

## Lift stories (`content.ts`, `ElevatorPlay.tsx`)

Picture stories of about two minutes in a house in cross-section with four floors and a lift. A thought bubble above the child shows what it needs next (the same drawing as the thing in the room), and the child rides the lift to that floor – big buttons 1–4 right of the house, a tap on the room, or the number keys and arrows. Nothing in the room is outlined or marked while the child rides there. A wrong floor: the child looks out and shakes its head (pose `shake`: the face slides from side to side so the head seems to turn, with swish lines, frowning brows and pressed lips), a light runs along the outline of the bubble and the right room starts to glow; after a while without a tap the same happens again. In the room the moves happen by themselves. There is no action button: only a move that matters for the story and can clearly be seen is wished for in the bubble, just like a floor, and the number of the floor the child is on lights up to be pressed again (or a tap on the room or the child) – cracking the two eggs, the mixer, each pancake, flushing the toilet, tidying the blocks, putting the boat into the puddle. Another number then: the child shakes its head („Erst hier!“). Three stories (`content.ts`): „Pfannkuchen“ (hungry: chef's hat, eggs from the fridge in the cellar, batter and pancakes in the kitchen, fetch the little brother, his juice, eat together on the roof terrace), „Gute Nacht“ (bath, pyjama, warm milk, brushing teeth, teddy, bed – the house goes dark) and „Pfützen“ (rain: boots, raincoat, umbrella, rain hat, paper boat, jump in the garden puddles until the rainbow comes). The children with their poses and clothes and the thought bubble are in `kid.tsx`, the things in `things.tsx`, the ten rooms in `rooms.tsx`, house, lift and floor buttons in `house.tsx`, sizes in `layout.ts`; the story runs as a script of waits in `ElevatorPlay.tsx`. All drawn in SVG, no emojis, nothing to read.

## Trail adventures („Weg-Abenteuer“, `trail/`)

A long world seen from the side that scrolls along with the child (far hills slower, for depth). Something the child loves goes away at the start (the kite flies off), and the child follows it. The action button lights up when the child can walk on: its frame turns yellow, the button red, it breathes and sends out rings. A tap on it, anywhere, or any key walks to the next station (after a while it jumps and the footprints and the ghost come again – never the goal: the thought bubble shows the goal only when it goes away). Before the tap the child can see what it will do: yellow footprints lead to the child's pale ghost, standing where the tap takes it and doing what it will do there (reaching for the thing, standing at the obstacle, on the top step), and the button shows the thing it gets there, or feet (walk to an obstacle or a moment), or stairs (a climb). A thing the tap picks up has a dashed yellow ring round it in the world, and waits pale in the backpack on the screen, ringed the same, over the place where it will go (`WantRing`). The child wears a red backpack (`Backpack` in `kid.tsx`, the `backpack` prop of `Kid`), and the same backpack is drawn big and open in the bag's frame on the screen: the things in it stand side by side (three at most), a long one like the plank slanted, each over a front pocket (with a buckle, or its small key cap on a computer). A thing lying on the way: the child takes it (pose `take`), holds it up high (`lift`), the backpack's lid swings open and the thing goes over the shoulder into it while the other hand reaches back (`pack`), the backpack bounces, and on the screen the thing drops into the big backpack. At an obstacle a thought bubble says why a thing is needed (`why` of the station): a creature thinks of what it wants (the ducks of the bread, the bubble above its head, growing away from the child), or the child thinks of what would happen (`worries.tsx`: wet under the sprinkler, in the water at the stream or the hole in the bridge, in the dark, the ball dug out of the sand, bare feet on hot sand) – a big frowning head, since the bubble is small. Then the things on the screen come up out of the backpack and the child taps the one that helps (or 1–3): it rises out of the big backpack, the child takes it out of its own backpack, holds it up and throws it over, and it is used by itself. A wrong thing is tried: the obstacle wobbles and tosses its head three times with a funny sound, the thing comes back into the hand and the backpack, and the right one glows (it also glows after a while without a tap, and a light runs along the `why` bubble). Food is eaten where it can be seen: in the mouth (`tl-chew`), or at the beak, bite by bite (`tl-bites`) – the ducks peck the bread up on the path and then waddle into the pond. The carried thing moves frame by frame in world points (`moveCarry` in `TrailPlay.tsx`), drawn in front of the child, or behind it while it sinks into the backpack or comes up out of it. Stairs take two taps, half of the steps each. At a fork (G-09) the way splits: two round picture signs on a signpost show the ways (stairs up, a tunnel), footprints lead to a ghost on each, and the child taps a sign or a ghost (or 1 / 2; any other tap or key makes the signs hop) – the action button stays dark, as there is nothing to walk on to yet. The picked way's first station starts right away, its own stations follow, and both ways meet again; next time the child can take the other one. At the end the goal is back in the hands, confetti, then „Nochmal“ and „Weiter“. Bag and button sit in framed boxes at the bottom of the screen (`layoutHud`): the backpack in a leather-coloured frame at the screen's left edge (clear of a notch), the button in its own frame. The button goes right next to the bag, left of the child, where one thumb reaches both: on a wide phone the picture leaves room at the sides for it; on a computer or tablet the child stands a little further right (the HUD's `kidX`, at most `KID_SHIFT` past `KID_AT`) so the button fits without covering it. Only if even that is too little does it go to the bottom-right corner; `hud.test.ts` checks this for computer, tablet and phone. On a computer (not on touch-only devices) small key caps show the keys: a space bar under the action button and 1, 2, 3 on the backpack's front pockets, under the things; a cap turns yellow while its key does something and goes down while the key is held. Any key still walks on – the space bar is only the one shown.
- `types.ts` – the level format: `TrailLevel` with its ground line, stations (`pickup`, `obstacle`, `climb` with an optional `slide` down, `moment` – something that happens by itself, and `fork` – two `ways`, each with its sign picture, its stations and optionally its own ground) and the goal's flights away and back. On water a level has a `raft`: the child stands on it from the start and paddles (pose `paddle`, a paddle on the button and the ghost) up to `raft.until`, a climb where it gets off and the raft stays behind. An obstacle with `float` sends the child slowly down ground points by itself after it is solved, holding the thing up (the umbrella over the waterfall), then puts the thing down at `put` (the level draws it there from then on); `levels.test.ts` checks both lie on the ground. `ground.ts` walks the ground and lists the paths through a level (`pathsOf`).
- `levels.ts` – the levels' data (`TRAIL_LEVELS`); `levels.test.ts` checks it along every path through the level (bag never over three, every need is in the bag, stations left to right, steps on the ground, the last station frees the goal), and that a way's own ground matches the level's outside the way.
- `<level>.tsx` – one world drawing per level (`drachen.tsx`, `ball.tsx`): a `Scenery` that draws the stations from `done`/`here`/`nope` (and `party`, true once the goal is back – the sandcastle gets built), a `Backdrop`, and optionally a `Front` drawn over the goal.
- What the child wears: `wear` of a level (bare feet at the beach) and of an obstacle that is solved by putting the thing on (the flip-flops on the hot sand) – the thing goes onto the child (`Outfit` in `kid.tsx`), not over the obstacle.
- `items.tsx` – the bag's things and the goals, drawn standing on (0, 0); a goal says how it is carried (on a string above the child, or in the hands) and whether it rolls. `TrailPlay.tsx` – the script; `trail.css` – classes `tl-` (`tl-ball-` for the ball level).
- The child (`Kid` from `kid.tsx`), the thought bubble and the action button (`house.tsx`) and their classes come from `elevator.css`, which the game always loads.

## Languages (German, English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, Russian)

The game follows the app language chosen on the home page (`useAppLanguage`). The stories and levels stay written in German (`content.ts`, `trail/levels.ts`); `en.ts`, `zh.ts`, `hi.ts`, `es.ts`, `ar.ts` and `ru.ts` are the phrasebooks – every spoken sentence and every story/level title, keyed by its German text – and `phrases.ts` (`PHRASEBOOKS`) looks a sentence up (`spokenIn` for speech, `titleIn` for titles). Speech always uses the voice of the language the words are in: a sentence missing from a phrasebook is said in German by the German voice. Small texts of the start screen and labels (`Stock 1`, `Los`, the key note) are inline `t({ de, en, zh, hi, es, ar, ru })`.
- New and changed sentences and titles come in German only; their lines in the phrasebooks are written only when the user asks for them. A level or story is translated whole or not at all: one whose title has no line in the app language is said all in German (`voice` in `TrailPlay.tsx`), shared lines like „Ab in den Rucksack!“ too, so a level never mixes two languages; `phrases.test.ts` checks a language's sentences per story and level, only where it has the title. When you change a German sentence that has translations, delete those lines (or update them, if you are translating anyway): `phrases.test.ts` fails on a left-over one, on a sentence missing in a language translated so far, and on Latin letters in Russian, Chinese, Hindi or Arabic. One German sentence has one line per language, so where the same German line is used for things of different gender, keep the translation gender-free („Берём с собой.“, „Let's take it along.“, „نأخذ هذا معنا.“).
- For the child: short, present tense, nothing that depends on the child being a boy or a girl – Russian „ты“/„мы“ without past-tense or adjective forms („Я хочу спать“, not „Я устал“), Spanish (Latin American) „Tengo sueño“, not „Estoy cansado“, Hindi „मुझे …“, requests or „let's“, Arabic noun phrases or „we“, never a command. Sound words as the language writes them („Кря-кря“, „Quack“, „嘎嘎“). Bello the dog is „Buddy“ / „豆豆“ / „शेरू“ / „Toby“ / „بيلو“ in English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish and Arabic.

## Rules for every story (from feedback; follow them in new stories and trail levels too)

- **The first thing in a room happens by itself.** Getting out of the lift, the child does the first move on its own (puts the hat on, opens the fridge, sits down) – the ride was the child's tap. A step whose whole point is one move needs no tap at all.
- **Only ask for moves that matter.** A move in the room is wished for (a task with `wish`) only when it really belongs to what the child is doing and can clearly be seen: cracking eggs, pouring a pancake, flushing, tidying. Waving, chewing, blowing, putting on, looking in the mirror are only shown – they do themselves. No wish while the child is hidden in the tub or the bed (`content.test.ts`).
- **A move in the room is asked like a floor.** The bubble shows the thing, and the number of the floor the child is on lights up to be pressed again – no extra action button in the lift stories (the trails keep theirs).
- **At most two presses in a row**, and several only where the child can count what each one does: two eggs, one press per pancake. Between runs something happens by itself (a task without `wish`, a `wait`, or a walk to a new `spot`). `content.test.ts` checks this.
- **Nothing in a room is framed.** No outline, dashed line or ring marks where the thing of the bubble stands (a dashed outline was tried and removed at the parent's wish): the bubble shows the thing, and after a wrong floor the right room glows. Nothing else is pointed at; in the room only the floor buttons light up.
- **A bubble picture shows the action, not only things.** „Jump into puddles“ is the child in boots in mid-air over a splashing puddle – a cloud over a flat puddle didn't say what it is about. A child drawn inside a picture keeps still (`el-pic-kid`).
- **Only wish for what can be seen.** The thing in the bubble stands visibly in its room – nothing in a cupboard, nothing to find in a second place, no trip to fetch something forgotten.
- **Things stand out from their room.** A thing to use has a colour (and a dark outline) that differs from the wall, worktop or furniture behind it – no blue bowl in front of blue cupboards, no white bag on white tiles.
- **Show what happens, with solid things.** What goes into a bowl, a pot or a puddle stays visible – in its opening, seen a little from above (the yolks, the milk, the flour heap, the mixer in the batter) – never through a see-through side: a glass bowl you could see through looked wrong and was made solid.
- **The thought bubble pops up once per wish.** Reminders don't pop it again; a light runs along its outline instead.
- **The bag is a backpack you can see working.** The child carries it on its back, and the one on the screen looks the same, open, with the things standing in it. Nothing jumps straight between the world and the corner: a thing goes into the child's hand, up high, over the shoulder into the backpack, and only then drops into the one on the screen – and comes out the same way back.
- **Things fit together.** What an animal wants is what it really eats or plays with (a dog a bone or a ball, a horse a carrot, ducks bread), and the thing comes from a place where it belongs: a dog wanting a pretzel from the bakery made no sense.
- **The button shows what it does before it is pressed** (trails): footprints to the child's ghost, the thing on the button. A press must never be a surprise.
- **Reminders show the next step, never the end goal** (trails). The goal far away doesn't help a 2-year-old waiting at a station; the button jumps and the footprints and the ghost come again. The goal is in the bubble only when it goes away.
- **A thing to pick up is clearly marked** (trails): a dashed yellow ring round it in the world, and the same ring round its pale picture in the backpack, where it will go.
- **Say why a thing helps** (trails): every obstacle has a `why` bubble – the creature thinks of the thing it wants (hungry ducks of bread), or the child of what would happen without it (it gets wet, it falls into the water, it can't see). The type requires it; `levels.test.ts` checks a creature's bubble is on the screen next to the child.
- **Animals move the way they look, and away from danger.** A creature drawn looking left that moves right is
  mirrored first (ducklings swimming backwards looked wrong), and nothing heads for a waterfall, a road or an edge
  when it leaves: the duck family goes up the far bank, not towards the falls.
- **What is used, is seen doing its job.** Food is eaten where it lies, bite by bite, never left lying untouched (bread just floating in the pond made no sense); a plank lies over the gap, the lantern lights the tunnel.
- **Bag and button are easy to find and reach.** Each in its own frame, the bag clearly a bag holding the things; at the screen's edges, not the picture's; on every screen (phone, tablet, computer) the button right next to the bag on the left – rather move the child a little to the right than put the button far away in the other corner. Key hints are small and under the things, not above them.

## What the parent wants next (read before making a level)

New levels of this game should be **trail adventures**, not more lift stories (keep the lift stories as they are):
- Point-and-click like Monkey Island, but for a 2-year-old: one tap does one thing, no inventory juggling.
- Anywhere, not only a house: stairs, nature, street, beach, snow, forest, water … A world that scrolls sideways (or up, for stairs and towers) is the default.
- The Disney-style bag: up to three things; at an obstacle the child is asked what to use (bag opens, voice asks if „Vorlesen“ is on), taps one, and the child uses it automatically. Mix in a thing that is not needed yet, so there is a real choice; every wrong try is funny, never an error.
- **No text anywhere the child looks: the child can't read.** Pictures, movement, sounds and the (optional) voice only. Titles exist only for the parents' start screen and screen readers.
- Shape of a level: a goal goes away at the start → 8–12 stations (pickups, obstacles, one climb) → the goal comes back → a small party. About 3–5 minutes.
- Make a new level with the skill `/neues-abenteuer` (`.claude/skills/neues-abenteuer/SKILL.md`).
- Ideas for further levels and mechanics are in `src/content/ideas/g-little-adventures.ts`, area „G“ (G-02 onward). Mark them done there when built.
