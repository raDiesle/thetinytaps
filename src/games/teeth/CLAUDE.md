# Zähne putzen

Route `/zaehne/`.

Brushing timer in seven steps, as long as its song (no choice of minutes: the song's own length, about two minutes), in KAI order (chewing surfaces, outside, inside). It starts with a short story of where the sugar monsters come from (`origin.tsx`, about 15 s, can be turned off): a child's face fills the screen, three sweets float big in the corners for a moment, then glide into the smiling mouth one by one, are chewed and leave sugar crumbs on the teeth, then the sun crosses the sky (evening: the day goes by) or the moon does and the sun comes up (morning: the night goes by) while each crumb grows into a monster, and the camera moves in from the head to the mouth (`.origin-camera`, a CSS zoom to `MOUTH_FOCUS`). The story's teeth stand in a scalloped gum too. The story, the sink and the mirror are one camera looking round the bathroom (`scenes.tsx`): from the face down to the sink, up into the mirror, and down again to tidy up – the old picture slides out while the new one slides in, never a cut. The child is happy about every sweet: its eyes follow it to its corner and down into the mouth, its brows go up and two hearts rise; while it chews its eyes close happily and its cheeks glow. Then the sink (`prep.tsx`, can be turned off): a fist (its wrist turned against the brush, so the arm always comes up from below) takes the brush (white and blue tufts, rubber grip) out of its cup (it clinks), the tube squeezes a pea-sized, striped dab of toothpaste onto the middle of the bristles; a chrome tap stands behind the basin. The sounds of the story and the sink are `Cue`s (`ORIGIN_CUES`, `PREP_CUES`, ms from the start of their part, `cues.ts`) that `TeethPlay` plays when the clock passes them and skips on a jump: a cheer (`yay`) when the sweets come, the bonbon's `wrapper`, a crunch (`lolly`) or `munch` and `chew` for each sweet, „Mmmh“ (`yum`) after the last, the brush `clink`ing in its cup – CC0 recordings in `src/lib/sounds/` with a synth fallback, loaded before the prelude. `prelude.test.ts` checks every cue lies inside its part and every recording has its file. A small 3D mouth model (`jaw.ts`) shows each step from the right angle – mouth open or teeth together, head turned to the side, the jaw being brushed tipped to show its top or tongue side. Arrows on the lips show when to open or close the mouth. The teeth grow out of the gum: the gum line (`GUM_LINE` in `jaw.ts`) rises into a papilla between neighbouring teeth and arches down over each crown, each tooth's faces end there, and the gum's walls reach a little over the crown's foot (`GUM_OVERLAP`) with a darker rim (`GUM_RIM`); each tooth is drawn between its own piece of gum's top and its walls, so it stands in the gum. The brush is a small 3D model too (`brush.tsx`: white and light-blue bristle tufts with a longer tip row, a rounded head, a thin neck, a green rubber grip; its foam grows over the first half minute), held as dentists teach it for each side (flat on the chewing surfaces, in the cheek pocket with the handle out of the corner of the mouth, tipped towards the gum inside) and glides along the row scrubbing, circling or wiping from gum to tooth. The brush is a layer of its own above the mirror (`.brush-layer`, an HTML layer with its own svg): React draws it only when it reaches the next tooth or turns, and the scrubbing, circling and wiping are CSS animations of that layer (`brush-swing`, `brush-wipe`), so a phone only moves a finished picture. There is no text on the picture at all, before, while or after brushing: only the picture, the controls (pause, „Von vorn“ – drawn signs, `ControlIcon`), the progress bar and the time right next to it (`.teeth-time`, hidden on narrow screens). The progress bar's first part is the time before the brushing (story, sink, first look in the mirror; lilac, `.teeth-segment.intro`), with a clock of its own in the same place: while it runs the time shows how long until the brushing starts (lilac pill), then the brushing time left. Sugar monsters sit on every tooth not brushed yet (only where the lip opening shows the tooth, see `insideBy` in `jaw.ts`), from the first look in the mirror; the ones to brush now glow, and each vanishes as the brush reaches it. The mirror (and the story and the sink before it) fills the whole screen behind the heading and the controls: `useRoom` measures `.teeth-room`, the free part between them, and `fitBox` (`mouth.tsx`) zooms so what matters just fits there and the rest of the screen shows more face or wall around it. For the mouth that is the lips with the arrows on them (`mouthFocus`), taken from the view of the moment, so a closed mouth or one seen from the side zooms in as far as an open one; the frame keeps its thickness in px. On a phone on its side the week chart waits for the end, so the sink keeps the room. The song always plays, no tapping needed, keeps the screen on; arrow keys skip steps, Space or the pause button next to the progress bar pauses, „Von vorn“ beside it starts again from the story, and tapping or dragging on the progress bar jumps to that point, the prelude too (also while paused; the bar sits above the pause screen). At the end (`Ending` in `TeethPlay.tsx`) it is unmistakable that the brushing is done: a gold medal (a smiling tooth on it, `medal.tsx`) swinging down at the top, without words, the clean grin with sparkling teeth, a fanfare and children cheering (`cheer`) for two bars; then the sink once more (`Prep` part `tidy`, 16 beats): the foamy brush is scrubbed under the running tap until the foam is gone, goes back into its cup with a rattle and a clink, and the hand lets go, with twinkles round the cup (`PREP_CUES.tidy`, played by the end's clock like the prelude's); then the grin again with the daytime's goodbye (spoken), the week chart's new star and the buttons – nothing else beside them (a cheering fox family stood there and was taken out, decoration a child wanted to tap, user feedback, November 2026). → skips to the buttons, Space starts again. The book on the shelf starts the brushing at once, with no start screen – its clock stands at the start (`held`, not paused) until the book lies open (`usePageStill`); ✕ and Esc go back to the shelf. Settings (`tinytaps.teeth.v3`) are only what is shown before (one choice for the story and the sink, taken at the start of each brushing so the clock never jumps) and the week chart, changed while playing behind ⚙️ held in the corner (`PlayOptions`); speech and sounds come from the home page.

Song (`music.ts`): `musik/musik-zaehneputzen-lied-<lang>.mp3`, one verse per step singing what the picture shows („Mund weit auf … hin und her“), the eighth part „Fertig! Blitzeblank“ on the end screen. It always plays, also when "🎵 Musik" is off on the home page: it is the brushing, not background music. It plays at the brushing time (from its first verse), so it follows pause and jumps. The voice says nothing during the steps and at the end, the song is the hint; only the prelude is spoken, and the song gets quiet while the voice speaks (it watches `talking()` of `src/lib/voice.ts`). Each step lasts as long as its verse (`sungSteps`, from the .json next to the song: where each verse begins), so the brushing lasts as long as the song (German 1:53, English 2:11, Russian 1:58, Spanish 1:49, Italian 1:52, Chinese 1:57, Hindi 2:11, Arabic 2:01); everything goes by `stepStarts`. The song starts loading as soon as the game is chosen in the menu (`preloadSong` in `TeethGame`, streamed so `useSongLoad` knows how far it is), and the story and the sink play while it loads. The brushing never begins without it: if it is still loading just before the countdown, the clock waits there (`Clock.until`, also at a point a grown-up jumps to) and a calm card says „🎵 Musik lädt …“ with a bar of how far it is, then goes on with 3, 2, 1. Only when the file can't be loaded at all, the synthesized `playTuneNote` tune plays and the voice says the steps. It begins with the first step. Before that, while the brush is fetched, the shared background song (`musik-hintergrund.mp3`) runs on from the menu (only while "🎵 Musik" is on); from the first step it is hushed (`useNoBackgroundMusic`), and back in the menu it goes on where it stopped. The last three seconds before the brushing are counted down big (3, 2, 1, under a drum roll that gets louder each second, `playDrumRoll`), and the brushing begins with „Los!“ and a „Tusch“ (`playTusch`) instead of the chime; during the whole prelude the time beside the progress bar shows the time until the start (lilac) instead of the brushing time.

The song is sung in the app language (🌐 on the home page, the language of the texts), German when there is no song in it (`sungLanguageFor` in `sung.ts`); there is no choice of song or language here. A language is just its two files: every `musik/musik-zaehneputzen-lied-<lang>.mp3` with its `.json` is found by its name (`sung.ts`); the lyrics for Suno are in `musik/suno-lied.md`. A new language: `/zahnputzlied`. The English song waits for the app to speak English.

Morning or evening (`daytime.ts`, by the clock: until 14:00 is the morning, no choice): in the morning the sun rises in the window at the sink and in the corner, and breakfast crumbs stick to the chin until all is clean; in the evening the moon is out, the sleeve is a pyjama, the sky turns dusk and the end says „Gute Nacht“. Every finished brushing puts a star on a week chart (`week.tsx`, Monday to Sunday, a sun and a moon slot per day), saved under `tinytaps.teeth.week.v1` for two weeks; today's column is bigger, says „Heute“ and is tinted yellow in the morning and violet in the evening, and the slot of this brushing has a ring. It is shown at the sink and at the end next to „Menü“ (`GameEnd menu`, a paper slip with the ribbon's shelf label: once brushed the child is done, so no „Nochmal“ – user request, November 2026 –, and there is no next level, so no „Weiter“; Space or „Von vorn“ still start again), never while brushing (can be turned off).

Languages: everything follows the app language (German, English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, Russian; `useAppLanguage`/`useT` from `@/lib/language`), the start screen as well as what the child sees and hears. The steps' German texts are in `steps.ts`, the translations in `steps.<language>.ts` by step id (`STEP_TRANSLATIONS`, `stepText(step, key, language)`); morning and evening in `daytime.ts` / `daytime.<language>.ts` (`DAYTIME_TRANSLATIONS`, `daytimeText`); the prelude, the end, the buttons and the picture labels inline as `{ de, en, zh, hi, es, ar, ru }`. The sugar monsters are sugar monsters, monstruos del azúcar, 糖怪兽, चीनी-मॉन्स्टर, وحوش السكر, сахарные монстры. Every spoken line goes through `spokenIn` (`src/lib/language.ts`): the line in the app language with its voice, or where it is missing the German line with the German voice, never German words in another voice. `texts.test.ts` checks that the translations that are there belong to a step or daytime text that exists, Russian, Chinese, Hindi and Arabic in their own script with no Latin letters, and that a text with Russian has the other five too; it does not ask a German-only text for any.

The Tagesplan (`/tagesplan/`) opens this game for its 🪥 card (`src/lib/routine.ts`): the brushing starts at once,
outside play time, and ✕ goes back to the plan, which the finished brushing ticks.

## Rules

- The child's face (the mirror in `mouth.tsx`, the story in `origin.tsx`) follows „Junge / Mädchen“ from the home page
  (`useFigure()`, F-04): the girl has two pigtails with yellow bands, and lashes at her eyes – in the mirror often only
  the eyes are in sight. Mouth, teeth and monsters are the same for both; the song and the lines say nothing of boy or girl.
- The monsters grow only in the story at the start. In the mirror they are already on the teeth: never let them pop up or arrive there (they only fade in softly as a side of the teeth turns into view), that would be a second, illogical origin.
- A monster sits on its tooth, not over its neighbours: keep `MONSTER_SIZE` (`mouth.tsx`) small, and the story's monsters about a tooth wide.
- The story is short (about 15 s): it explains, the brushing is the game.
- Every picture before the brushing flows into the next (`Scenes`): no cut, the camera slides or zooms (two beats, `SCENE_MS`).
- The end must say „you did it“ without reading: a picture of success (medal, cheer, confetti) right when the brushing ends, then the routine is finished as at home (spit out, rinse the brush, put it back in its cup) before the buttons come. Keep the end about as long as the prelude parts, not longer than the sung finale.
- Pictures are cute and use the whole screen: big round head, big eyes with shines, soft colours; zoom (`FOCUS`) onto what matters instead of drawing it small in the middle.
- What the child should see stays still long enough to be seen (at least a bar, 2.14 s) before it moves on; nothing whizzes across.
- Every motion goes with the song (112 BPM): durations are beats (0.536 s), two beats or bars, and nothing loops faster than a beat (a tapped giggle may be quicker). Write new timings that way (`STROKE_S` in `brush.tsx`, the beat comment in `teeth.css`).
- No words on the picture, ever – no heading („Süßes essen“, „Geschafft!“), no hint for grown-ups („Erbsengroß“, „Ausspucken“), no goodbye line (user feedback, November 2026): the picture, the song and the voice say it, the medal says „well done“. The names of the prelude parts and the steps live in the progress bar's tooltips. `controls.test.ts` checks.
- The controls are drawn signs (`ControlIcon`), never emoji (⏸️ ▶️ looked poor, user feedback, November 2026); the time before brushing shows where the brushing time does, as the first part of the progress bar with its own clock, never in a corner of its own.
- Everything follows the clock, also after a jump on the progress bar: the picture plays on from the point tapped,
  never from the start of its part (user feedback, November 2026). The story's and the sink's pictures are CSS
  animations that start with their part (`at-<part>`), so after every jump `TeethPlay` sets them to where the clock is
  in it (`jumps`, `getAnimations`); a new animation there belongs to its part too, never runs on from an earlier one. Their pause
  rules are `!important`, since a part's rule with the `animation` shorthand is more specific and kept them running.
  `controls.test.ts` checks.
- The toothpaste is a pea-sized dab, as much as a child's brush gets – never a stripe along the whole head (user feedback, November 2026).
- A monster only sits where its tooth shows through the lip opening, at least `LIP_EDGE` inside it (`insideBy`, tested in `jaw.test.ts`); never on the lips. And the teeth being brushed always show whole, with their monsters: seen from the side the near corner of the mouth would hide the back teeth, so the corners are pulled open sideways as far as the step needs, as the brush pulls a cheek (`openCorners`, `TOOTH_CLEAR` in `jaw.ts`; `jaw.test.ts` checks every step – the corner once hid them, user feedback, November 2026). Never draw the lips so a step's teeth hide behind them.
- The week chart always makes clear which day it is and whether it is morning or evening. The days' names are a little
  bigger than their sun and moon (`.teeth-day-name`) in broad columns, never small print under them, and today stands
  out at a glance – much bigger, lifted, a strong colour, a broad frame and a big „Heute“ – yet still, never pulsing
  (user feedback, November 2026, asked twice).
- Translations are made only when the user asks (root `CLAUDE.md`): a new text comes in German only (`{ de: "…" }`, nothing in `steps.<language>.ts` / `daytime.<language>.ts`), and a German text whose meaning changes loses its translations instead of keeping a stale one; the gap goes into `src/content/ideas/languageStatus.ts`. When a translation is asked for: short, warm, present tense, "ты"/tú/तुम, no past tense or gendered words about the child ("Сахарных монстров больше нет", not "убежали"); Hindi uses commands and "let's" (चलो), Arabic "we" (هيّا ن…).
- Say every line through `spokenIn`/`stepText`/`daytimeText` and `say`, which plays its ElevenLabs recording (`stimme/`, Tapsi's voice, October 2026; where the language has no recording of a line its English one plays, else nothing – `spokenIn` carries the English text as `english`); two lines that may be in different languages are said one after the other (`queue`), never joined into one string. A line that is not queued cuts off what is being said, so an effect that says something only on a change (going on after a pause) must not say it when the game mounts: that once cut off „Guten Morgen!“ before the first cue. A new or changed line is listed for recording by `src/content/recordedLines.ts` and stays silent until `bun run voice make --only zaehne` (only when the user asks).
- Never switch the song in, or decode it, while brushing: a song that arrives mid-brushing makes the picture stutter on phones. It loads from the start screen on, and the clock waits for it before the countdown (with „Musik lädt …“ and a bar when it is late); the stand-in tune is only for a song that can't load.
- 3D parts drawn as flat faces are stacked by the planes between them, never by sorting their middles: a small part next to a long one (a bristle tuft at the far end of the head) can have its middle nearer and show through the plastic. The brush's boxes all lie apart along one of its axes (`drawOrder` in `brush.tsx`, checked in `brush.test.ts`); a new part has to as well.
- The mirror is pictures on top of each other (`mouth.tsx`), each a layer of its own (`.mouth-moving`) over the still one (face, teeth, gums), which is built once per view (`useMemo`): the glow on the teeth to brush, an svg of its own that pulses as a whole (its opacity, which a phone does without painting); the lips over the glow and the waiting monsters, which stand still; the monsters being brushed now, the arrows on the lips and the end's sparkles, each a small HTML layer of its own (`Sprite`, `.mouth-sprite`) whose sway, tremble, glow, nudge and twinkle move the HTML round its little svg (`.sprite-body`, `.sprite-glow`, `.tooth-sparkle > svg`), never something inside an svg; the mirror's glass and frame, drawn in screen px (its own viewBox, which never changes, so a turning head doesn't paint it again); then the brush. A CSS animation inside an svg has the phone paint that svg again in every frame, and even small ones kept its main thread busy: never animate anything inside an svg here (the glow once pulsed in one svg with the lips, the monsters swayed and blinked in one the size of the screen, the face blinked in the still picture – each had the phone paint the screen anew many times a second). So nothing blinks in the mirror. Never put something that changes with the clock into the still picture, and keep what the clock passes to the moving parts the same from tick to tick (`PlacedMonster` is `memo`, `tickle` a `useCallback`, places rounded): redrawing the whole mouth with every 200 ms tick made the brushing stutter on phones.
- The brush's shape is drawn for the stop it is nearest and only turns when it reaches the next one (then it settles, rounded, so a brush holding still writes nothing to the page); gliding between stops only moves its layer. Its layer is only as big as the brush (`reach` in `brush.tsx`): a layer the size of the screen had a phone paint whole bands of the screen again each time the brush turned.
- Phones must keep up (`bun run perf zaehne` stays within its budget on the old Android; run it after changing what
  moves). What moves on every frame is an HTML layer moved by CSS (`transform`, `opacity`): the brush's strokes, the
  scene slides, the story's camera. Never redraw the brush, a monster or a picture per frame from React, and never
  animate an SVG attribute or a big svg's group. Only the monster being brushed now moves; the waiting ones stand
  still (no blink, no sway). The one per-frame redraw is the mirror while the head turns or the mouth opens (`gliding`): keep it
  cheap – the model is built once per step of opening (`mouthAt`), points are formatted by rounding (`fmt`), and a
  monster's drawing is kept while only its place changes (`PlacedMonster`). Every layer with the picture's viewBox is
  painted whole in each of those frames, so as few as possible are drawn then: the glow on the teeth waits until the
  head holds still, and the lips with the waiting monsters join the still picture meanwhile (one screen per frame
  instead of four, November 2026). Nothing that stays put on the screen (the mirror's frame and glass) takes the
  picture's viewBox.
- The story (`origin.tsx`) is still pictures – the sky behind, the child – with every moving thing between and on them
  an HTML layer of its own (`Piece`, `.origin-piece` inside `.origin-space`, which is picture units on the screen): the
  sun or the moon, the brows, pupils, happy eyes, cheeks, the chewing mouth with its crumbs and monsters, the hearts and
  the sweets. A piece draws in picture units and its keyframes stay those of the one picture; its `area` is where it
  lies, and a transform origin is the middle of that area or set on it. All of them once moved inside the one picture
  the size of the screen, and „Süßes“ painted 36–38 screens a second (budget 30). A new moving thing in the story is a
  `Piece` too; only the sky, alone in the svg behind, changes as a whole. `layers.test.ts` checks.
- Animate only with keyframes this game defines (`teeth-…`) or the shared ones in `src/styles/animations.css` (`pop-in` …); another game's keyframes only exist after that game was opened (`css.test.ts` checks).
- The song is the brushing, not background music: it sings the steps and sets how long they last, so it always plays, also when "🎵 Musik" is off, and there is no "plain music" or "no song" choice.
- What the app already decides is never a setting here: the song's language is the app language (the language of the texts), sounds and voice are no switches at all (root `CLAUDE.md`), the daytime is the clock. Never add a second choice for the same thing.
- Settings are few, with short labels that start with an emoji; options that belong together are one `Choice` („Vorher zeigen“: 🍬 + 🪥 / 🍬 / 🪥 / ⏩), explained in one short `setting-note` below.
